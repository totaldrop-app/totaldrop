# 0013: No account is kept on the server: the Ghost ring

- **Status:** Accepted, 4 November 2026 (Ozan, #243).
  Supersedes [0004](0004-mullvad-tarzi-hesap.md).

## Context

In 0004, an account was a 16-digit number generated by the server. In that
design the server kept the device list. The 24-hour wait for a device added
with the number also depended on the server's honesty. Yet the
[threat model](../tehdit-modeli.md) treats the servers as untrusted. The
number was also a bearer secret: whoever knew the number could do
everything.

On 30 October 2026 Ozan chose a "device ring" model instead of the number.
On 4 November 2026 he asked for a more advanced and more secure system.

## Options considered

The options were evaluated against Signal, Mullvad, Session, Briar,
SimpleX, Keybase, passkeys, MLS and the MLS Virtual Clients draft. This
evaluation, and the design written after it, went through five independent
review rounds.

1. **Witnessed ring:** a signed device chain and a transparency log. An
   account record stays on the server.
2. **Ghost ring (Hayalet halka):**
   - there is no account table on the server;
   - mailbox names rotate every day;
   - contacts send to the ring's head and do not see the number of
     devices;
   - recovery is delayed.
3. **Full MLS ring:** a standard, but heavier than needed for n ≤ 16
   devices.

## Decision

Option 2, the Ghost ring. The details are in [hesap.md](../hesap.md). The
binding points are:

- **Server:** keeps no account, device list or contact list. It only knows
  short-lived mailboxes with random names.
- **Chain:** membership is a chain signed by the devices. The ring secret is
  renewed on every change. Ring heads have a lifetime of at most 7 days.
- **Transparency log:** an anonymous transparency log with independent
  witnesses is there **in the first release** (Ozan, 4 November 2026). The
  witnesses also enforce inclusion: they do not sign a root that has left
  out, for more than 1 hour, a record the ring sent them (Ozan, 4 November
  2026). Both the sending and the receiving side check freshness.
- **Recovery:**
  - is optional and made of 24 words;
  - the waiting period is 72 hours with 2 or more devices, 7 days with a
    single device;
  - the recovery key can only add a device and resolve a fork;
  - the monthly recovery record holds only the latest head, not the
    contact list;
  - contacts compare the words again; there is no automatic acceptance.
- **Identity and names:** no identity is written on any device. The device
  and contact lists are the same on every device. The user gives the names,
  and the names never leave the ring.
- **Tiers and approval:**
  - There are three trust tiers: my devices, known contacts, nearby.
  - Management operations need a separate management key and user
    verification.
  - Strict mode (approval by two devices, a second factor in recovery) is a
    setting and is written into the chain; contacts check it too.
- **Against abuse:** ARC tokens, proof of work and an IP limit (Ozan,
  4 November 2026).
- **Android notifications:** UnifiedPush and a connection while the app is
  open. A content-free FCM wake-up is optional (Ozan, 4 November 2026).
- **Limits:** at most 16 devices. OHTTP and Tor come in a later stage.
- **The choice between privacy and auditability:** contacts see in which
  epoch the membership changed (not the number of devices). This was
  accepted deliberately, so that contacts can check the rules for adding
  and removing.

## Why

- **Data that is not kept cannot leak.** Even if the server database leaks,
  no accounts, device counts or contact relations come out of it without
  the keys.
- **The security claims are aimed at a malicious server too.** The parts
  that are to achieve this are the transparency log, the freshness check
  before sending and the contacts' re-verification. Every critical finding
  in the five review rounds followed the pattern "bad server + old
  authority". The design brings an answer to each; that these are enough
  will only be shown by the formal model and an independent cryptography
  review. The remaining corner cases of the fork and recovery rules are not
  patched by hand; they are settled in the Tamarin model
  ([hesap.md](../hesap.md), "Rules to settle in the formal model").
- **Only standard parts are used.** The algorithms sit behind the version
  field; if a weakness is found, they can be replaced.

## Consequences

- **Documents:** [hesap.md](../hesap.md) was rewritten. The status of 0004
  became "superseded". An account section was added to the
  [threat model](../tehdit-modeli.md).
- **Order of verification and code:** before any code is written, the
  Tamarin model and an independent human cryptography review are done.
  Then come the protocol notes, the core, the server and the apps. Each is
  a separate issue.
- **Size of the work:** About 2–2.5 times the witnessed ring. The reasons
  are the log, the relations with the witnesses and the verification flows
  on the contacts' side.
- **Wording:** the texts on the site, in the stores and in the privacy
  policy do not say "a world first". For the account feature they say "the
  server keeps no account". The privacy policy is updated in 35 languages
  before the feature is released.
- **Remaining risks:** IP and timing, the moment a credential is obtained
  in ARC, the phone and the phrase being stolen together, and library
  maturity. All are written down in [hesap.md](../hesap.md).

## Review conditions

This decision is evaluated again if one of the following happens:

- The Tamarin model or the independent review refutes a property.
- MLS Virtual Clients becomes an RFC and a space shared by several rings
  (family groups) is wanted.
- One of the post-quantum Rust libraries reaches an audited 1.0 release
  (the library choice is then made again).
