Roadmap · updated 11 November 2026

What is coming to TotalDrop

Dates are aims, not promises: a feature ships only when it is safe. Every step can be followed in the open repository.

The order we work in

  1. Stability

    Every transfer arrives, on any network and through a dropped connection.

  2. The optional account

    Your own devices and saved contacts, without codes and without accounts on our servers.

  3. More platforms

    Windows, iPhone, iPad and Mac.

A new idea joins this order; it does not jump ahead of it.

Done

October and November 2026

  • An open protocol

    TotalDrop Protocol v0: transfer codes with CPace, Noise XXpsk3 with post-quantum ML-KEM-768, QUIC with single-use keys. Written down so anyone can build a compatible app.

  • Command-line client

    Send and receive from a terminal on Linux, macOS and Windows.

    Linux · macOS · Windows

  • Android beta

    Send and receive with a code, in your language, following the phone's colours. On Google Play by invitation.

    Android

  • Linux app

    Qt 6 and native Wayland. Follows the theme of KDE Plasma, Hyprland and Omarchy live. Packages for Arch, Debian, Ubuntu and Fedora.

    Linux

  • Through firewalls

    Both devices listen and dial, so a firewall on one side no longer stops a transfer.

  • Across the internet

    Devices find their public address with STUN and connect directly through most home routers. When that is impossible, the encrypted traffic goes through our relay on Cloudflare, which cannot read it.

  • Privacy mode in the command line

    Relay only: the other side never learns your IP address.

    Linux · macOS · Windows

  • Privacy mode in the apps

    One switch in Settings: everything goes through the relay, so the other device never learns your IP address.

    Android · Linux

  • Linux on ARM

    .deb and .rpm packages for ARM64 computers such as Snapdragon X and NVIDIA RTX Spark.

    Linux

  • In your language

    The apps and this site in 35 languages, with your region's number formats. Native speakers are checking the drafts.

  • Send text and the clipboard

    Send a note, a link or what you copied. It arrives as text you can copy, not as a file.

    Android · Linux #313 #326

  • Direct connections more often

    IPv6 paths, and on Linux a port opened on your home router only for the transfer and closed after it, so fewer transfers need the relay. In the code; it arrives with the next releases.

    All platforms #403 #406

Now

November 2026

  • Steadier transfers

    A transfer should keep going when the phone leaves the screen, the laptop wants to sleep or only the relay gets through.

    Done: a short network drop no longer ends a transfer, and Android asks once to keep TotalDrop running in the background. Next: background and sleep on every platform, and steadier, faster relay-only transfers.

    All platforms #340 #185

  • Send from where you are

    Send without opening TotalDrop first: from the share menu, a text selection or the file manager.

    Done: share menu, text selection, clipboard and icon shortcuts on Android; right-click in Dolphin, Nautilus and Nemo on Linux. Next: a Quick Settings tile, receive links that open the app, and KDE's Share menu.

    Android · Linux #53 #54

  • Scan the QR code with the camera

    Point the phone at the sender's screen instead of typing the code.

    Not started yet.

    Android #29

  • Receive in the browser

    Open a link, type the code, save the files. Nothing to install, still end-to-end encrypted.

    Not started yet.

Next

Winter 2026–27

  • Resume interrupted transfers

    A dropped connection picks up where it stopped. The receiver keeps what already arrived, so sending again sends only what is missing; later it reconnects on its own.

    Started: design accepted (decision 0020).

    All platforms #31

  • Switch from relay to direct

    A transfer that started through the relay moves to a direct path as soon as one opens, and reacts at once when the network changes instead of waiting for silence.

    All platforms #32

  • Huge folders, little memory

    The list of files is read piece by piece, so even a folder with a very long list needs little memory on the receiving side.

    All platforms #47

  • Flathub and Steam Deck

    TotalDrop on Flathub, and tested on Steam Deck in Desktop Mode and Game Mode. The same Linux app, not a separate one.

    Done: the Flatpak is built in our CI. Next: the Flathub submission.

    Linux · Steam Deck #295

  • Package managers

    Ready-built command-line programs from our CI, then Homebrew, Scoop, the AUR and winget.

    Designed (decision 0015).

    Linux · macOS · Windows #34 #344

Later

    Designed (decision 0013), and six formal models of it are checked with the Tamarin prover. Next: an independent review by a cryptographer, then the server and the apps.

After the audit

  • Independent security audit

    An outside firm reviews the protocol, the cryptography and the code that handles untrusted input. The full report is published.

    All platforms #40

  • A formal model of the handshake

    A ProVerif or Tamarin proof that the hybrid handshake keeps its promises.

    All platforms #41

  • Post-quantum signatures

    Device keys add ML-DSA-65 next to Ed25519, so even a future quantum computer cannot impersonate you.

    All platforms #42

  • Community relays

    People who opt in can use relays run by others, from a signed list.

    All platforms #43

Not planned

What we decided not to do

  • Sending from a browser

    Sending always starts in a TotalDrop app. Receiving in the browser is still planned.

    #221

  • Tapping phones (NFC)

    It would only help between two phones. Scanning the QR code does the same on every device.

  • Swarms like BitTorrent

    They help many people share one file, not a private transfer between two devices, and they show strangers what is shared and from which IP address.
