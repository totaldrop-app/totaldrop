Less, but better

TotalDrop follows Dieter Rams: every element has a job, and whatever has none is left out.

  • Part of the system

    The apps take their colours, light or dark theme, icons and windows from the system, and on Linux the fonts too. Only the mark and its colours stay the same everywhere.

  • One accent per view

    Violet marks the one thing to do next, links and the focus ring. Everything else is ink on paper.

  • No effects

    No shadows, gradients, glass or glow. Surfaces are told apart by a hairline or a quieter tone; size and position make the hierarchy.

  • Honest and plain

    Short sentences that say what happens. No exclamation marks, no invented numbers, no promises the code does not keep.

The mark

The mark is called Köşe, Turkish for corner: a circle with one square corner. The circle is the whole (Total); the corner turns it into a drop (Drop) and points where the file goes. It is also the corner of a file and of a device's screen.

In right-to-left languages the layout mirrors, but the mark does not: at rest its corner is always at the top right.

Motion

While a transfer runs, the mark turns clockwise a quarter at a time around the centre of its circle, and its corner points at each side in turn: from every device to every device. The website, the Android app and the Linux app use the same values.

Cycle
3.2 seconds, repeating: four quarters, back where it began.
Each quarter
0.8 seconds: a 90° turn in 0.6 seconds, then a 0.2 second rest.
Easing
cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)
Reduced motion
When the system asks for less motion, the mark stays at rest and progress is shown by the bar and the percentage.

Turn Rest

This is the brand's only animation. Screens move with the platform's own transitions: no bounce, sparkle or confetti.

What not to do

The mark is one flat colour, used as it is. These are the common mistakes.

  • Don't

    Round the corner.

  • Don't

    Stretch the circle into an ellipse.

  • Don't

    Put it in a frame, ring or box.

  • Don't

    Paint it another colour.

  • Don't

    Add a gradient, shadow or outline.

  • Don't

    Turn it at rest: the corner stays at the top right.

  • Don't

    Mirror it, also not for right-to-left languages.

  • Don't

    Place it on a busy image or a ground with too little contrast.

Do not put platform or manufacturer logos (Apple, Android, Windows) next to the mark; use plain device drawings instead.

Type

Two open typefaces under the SIL Open Font License, served from this site. Where a script is not covered, the system's own font takes over.

Aa

Bricolage Grotesque

Headings, codes, step numbers and the wordmark, in bold and extra bold, never under 18 pixels. Large headings are set tight.

Aa

Atkinson Hyperlegible

Everything else: running text, buttons and fields. Made by the Braille Institute so that similar letters such as I, l and 1 stay apart.

  1. h1 Bricolage 800 · clamp(2.6rem, 6.4vw, 4.75rem) · −0.035emEvery device to every device
  2. h2 Bricolage 800 · clamp(1.75rem, 3.6vw, 2.5rem) · −0.025emHow it works
  3. h3 Bricolage 700 · 1.25remShare the code
  4. body Atkinson 400 · 1.0625rem · 1.6Files go from your device to theirs. No copy waits on a company's server, and no link expires in a week.
  5. small Atkinson 700 · 0.875rem · +0.06emFree · Open source · Beta

Lines of running text stay under 44rem. Figures that line up, such as sizes and percentages, use tabular numbers. Letter spacing is removed in Arabic-script languages, where it would break the joins.

Buttons and components

A button says what it does, in one or two words. On this website the buttons are drawn as below; in the apps they are the platform's own (Material 3 on Android, the desktop's buttons on Linux), with the same rules.

Three kinds of button

States

Size
56 pixels tall, 24 pixels of space at each side; as wide as the label, or the full width of a phone screen for the main action in the apps.
Shape
On this website, fully rounded ends. No shadow, gradient or glow.
Label
Atkinson Hyperlegible Bold, 16 pixels, in sentence case. A verb first: "Choose files", "Download the beta".
Side by side
A small gap apart, the main action first; on a narrow screen they wrap onto the next line.
  • Never two filled buttons in one view.
  • No button with only an icon and no word.
  • No capitals, exclamation marks or vague labels such as "OK" or "Click here".
  • A link that takes you somewhere looks like a link; a button does something.

Icons

  • In the apps, icons come from the system: Material icons on Android, the desktop's icon theme on Linux (Breeze on Plasma, symbolic icons elsewhere).
  • Icons take the colour of the text next to them; only status icons use the success or error colour.
  • An icon is never the only label: a word goes with it.
  • Drawings on the website are plain lines on a 24-unit grid, 1.75 units thick, with round ends, like these devices.
  • Icons that point a way, such as a back arrow, mirror in right-to-left languages; the mark never does.

Voice

  • Say what happens

    A button names its action: "Choose files", "Accept". An error says what went wrong and what to do next, without apologising.

  • Sentence case, calm tone

    Only the first word and names start with a capital. No capitals for emphasis, no emoji, no exclamation marks.

  • Words that are true

    "End-to-end encrypted" only where it is. A privacy promise says the same as the privacy policy, and limits are written where people see them.

  • In 35 languages

    Every text a person reads is written for all of TotalDrop's languages, with local number formats. Transfer codes and check words stay in English, so both devices show the same words.
